## 1. ESPR textiles: Working Plan priority, delegated act status, expected requirements and DPP data, when a DPP becomes mandatory

### Takeaway
No textiles/apparel ecodesign + DPP delegated act exists as of 2026-11-09: no draft has been published or consulted on, and the Commission's own DPP page gives "Q4 2027" as the planned adoption date. With ESPR's normal minimum lead time of 18 months, a mandatory apparel DPP is realistically not before ~mid-2029. The horizontal DPP infrastructure (registry, standards) is being put in place in 2026.

### Cited Findings
- Commission DPP textile-apparel page: textile apparel is a priority product group in the ESPR Working Plan 2025-2030; textile-specific DPP requirements will be set "in the same future delegated act" that introduces ecodesign requirements; timeline item "Q4 2027: Planned adoption of the ESPR Delegated Act for textiles"; caveat "Implementation timelines may evolve" — [European Commission, DG GROW, DPP textile apparel](https://single-market-economy.ec.europa.eu/single-market/digital-product-passport/textile-apparel_en) (fetched), acc. 2026-11-09.
- Same page, expected DPP information (final list to be fixed by the delegated act): product identification and characteristics; fibre composition (per Textile Labelling Regulation); information on use, repair, maintenance; information on reuse, resale, disassembly, refurbishment, disposal, recycling; origin information; identification of relevant economic operators. Access via online points of sale and a data carrier on the product (e.g., QR code). Obligated: economic operators placing on the market (manufacturers, importers); distributors/dealers must ensure DPP is available — [European Commission DPP textile apparel](https://single-market-economy.ec.europa.eu/single-market/digital-product-passport/textile-apparel_en) (fetched), acc. 2026-11-09.
- Same page: the DPP proposal draws on a JRC technical study (with stakeholder consultation) supporting the impact assessment, using the JRC methodology for DPP data requirements; links a "Have Your Say" initiative "Ecodesign requirements for sustainable and circular apparel textiles" with no date given — [European Commission DPP textile apparel](https://single-market-economy.ec.europa.eu/single-market/digital-product-passport/textile-apparel_en) (fetched), acc. 2026-11-09.
- ESPR Working Plan is COM(2025) 187; textiles are a top-priority category; status "pre-draft, no draft act published"; no textiles draft delegated act published or consulted on; JRC preparatory studies are the only public material; footwear is a separate, deferred category; first category act consultation was iron & steel (20 May-12 Aug 2026), with iron & steel adoption indicated Q4 2026; textiles adoption Q4 2027; indicative compliance "mid-2029 or later" based on ESPR minimum 18-month lead time. Page "last verified 10 August 2026" — [ESPR Atlas, Textiles](https://espratlas.com/sectors/textiles/) (fetched; secondary tracker), acc. 2026-11-09. Working Plan text: [EUR-Lex COM(2025) 187](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/PDF/?uri=CELEX:52025DC0187) (cited by ESPR Atlas, not fetched).
- Expected DPP data per JRC (subject to change): item-level traceability/serialisation; fibre composition and recycled fibre content; microplastics release potential; disassembly and recycling instructions; chemical substances of concern; a JRC May 2026 study proposes four content categories (identification, producer information, product information, compliance documentation), role-based access, applying to products >=80% textile fibre by weight — [ESPR Atlas, Textiles](https://espratlas.com/sectors/textiles/) (fetched), citing [JRC product bureau, product group 467 documents](https://susproc.jrc.ec.europa.eu/product-bureau/product-groups/467/documents) (not fetched), acc. 2026-11-09.
- Secondary sources expect requirements on durability, repairability, recycled content, hazardous substances, carbon footprint; one source cites initial recycled-content thresholds of 10-25% — no official draft supports this figure — [Carbonfact ESPR textile](https://www.carbonfact.com/blog/policy/espr-textile); [Caninde](https://caninde.eu/articles/espr-textiles-seven-impacts/) (search), acc. 2026-11-09.
- Dated tracker log (6 Nov 2026 / 3 Oct 2026): textiles/apparel are the first-priority final product group with indicative delegated act in 2027 / adoption "fourth quarter of 2027", status pending; DPP registry launched 20 Jul 2026 (legal deadline 19 Jul 2026) with testing environment; DPP Registry Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/1778 (applies 6 Aug 2026; operators must be "verified economic operators" via eID, registration includes commodity code); DPP harmonised standards decision (EU) 2026/1736 with EN 18216:2026, EN 18219:2026, EN 18220:2026, EN 18221:2026 applying from 15 Jul 2026; DPP service-provider delegated act (Art. 11) indicative Q4 2026 — [Textile School compliance change log](https://www.textileschool.com/compliance/changes/) (fetched; secondary aggregator, contains at least one evident error on EUDR, see Q7), acc. 2026-11-09.
- Contradicting/outdated claim: a Sept 2025 law-firm note says "Textiles must carry a digital record from 2027" — [CMS, 18 Sep 2025](https://cms.law/en/gbr/legal-updates/eu-parliament-approves-new-measures-on-textile-waste-what-manufacturers-retailers-and-e-commerce-sellers-need-to-know) (fetched), acc. 2026-11-09; contradicted by the Commission's Q4 2027 *adoption* date above.

### Inferences
- Earliest realistic mandatory apparel DPP: adoption Q4 2027 + >=18 months = ~Q2-Q3 2029 at the earliest; could slip (Commission itself hedges). Any product claiming "EU DPP compliant" before then is voluntary/pilot.
- The data a Bangladeshi exporter will most likely be asked for by EU buyers ahead of the act: fibre composition (already mandatory under TLR), recycled-content share, substances of concern (REACH-linked), country/facility of manufacture (origin + operator identification), care/repair info, and a unique product/batch identifier. Carbon footprint is discussed by secondary sources but not listed on the Commission page.
- Legal obligation sits on the EU importer/brand ("placing on the market"), not the Bangladeshi factory; the factory's obligation will be contractual (data supply).

### Gaps
- No official draft text of the textiles delegated act; exact information requirements, thresholds, and whether carbon footprint/social criteria are included are unknown.
- Could not fetch EUR-Lex directly (EUR-Lex pages returned empty); regulation numbers 2026/1778 and 2026/1736 come only from a secondary tracker and were not verified on EUR-Lex.
- Exact Working Plan adoption date (believed to be April 2025) not verified in this session.
- Whether the Commission's 2027 work programme (published 4 Nov 2026) changes the textiles timing was not checked.

## 2. ESPR ban on destroying unsold apparel and footwear; disclosure duties

### Takeaway
ADOPTED and APPLYING: since 19 July 2026 large enterprises may not destroy unsold apparel, clothing accessories and footwear (medium from 19 July 2030; micro/small exempt). Derogations and a standard disclosure format were adopted by the Commission on 9 Feb 2026; the standardized annual disclosure (CN code, number/weight discarded, reason, waste route) applies from Feb/Mar 2027.

### Cited Findings
- Commission adopted on 9 Feb 2026 a Delegated Regulation C(2026) 659 (derogations) and an Implementing Regulation C(2026) 660 (disclosure format). Ban + derogations apply to large companies from 19 July 2026; medium-sized companies "in 2030"; standardised disclosure reporting applies "from February 2027"; "an estimated 4-9% of unsold textiles are destroyed before they are even worn" — [EU Circular Economy Stakeholder Platform news, 11 Feb 2026](https://circulareconomy.europa.eu/platform/en/news-and-events/all-news/new-eu-rules-stop-destruction-unsold-clothes-and-shoes) (fetched), acc. 2026-11-09. Commission document: [C(2026) 659](https://ec.europa.eu/transparency/documents-register/api/files/C(2026)659_0/090166e528eb6d75) (search), acc. 2026-11-09.
- Derogations (destruction still permitted) where the product: is "dangerous" under GPSR (EU) 2023/988; is non-compliant/unfit for purpose and destruction is legally required or the appropriate corrective action; infringes IP rights; is subject to a licence restricting sale after a set period; is unacceptable for consumer use due to damage, deterioration or contamination (incl. hygiene); was offered for donation to at least three EU social economy entities, or on an accessible web page for at least eight weeks, without acceptance — [Baker McKenzie, Feb 2026](https://www.bakermckenzie.com/en/insight/publications/2026/02/european-union-european-commission-adopts-new-espr-measures) (fetched), acc. 2026-11-09.
- Disclosure: large companies covered from the first full financial year after ESPR entry into force (18 Jul 2024); medium from 19 Jul 2030. Standard format data per product category: CN code; number and weight of units discarded (incl. packaging weight); reason for discarding; proportion sent to preparation for reuse, recycling, other recovery, disposal; measures taken/planned to prevent destruction. Standard format applies to products discarded from the first full financial year after the act's application date (Feb 2027). Penalties set by Member States — [Baker McKenzie, Feb 2026](https://www.bakermckenzie.com/en/insight/publications/2026/02/european-union-european-commission-adopts-new-espr-measures) (fetched), acc. 2026-11-09.
- Act numbers cited by secondary sources: Delegated Regulation (EU) 2026/296 (derogations) and Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/2 (disclosure format, applicable 2 March 2027); one source gives OJ publication of 2026/296 as 22.4.2026; another says derogation use requires an outbound statement to the waste operator under Art. 4 of 2026/296 — [ESPR Atlas](https://espratlas.com/sectors/textiles/) (fetched); [Textile School log](https://www.textileschool.com/compliance/changes/) (fetched); [myproductpassport.eu](https://myproductpassport.eu/blog/new-eu-regulation-ecodesign-requirements-2026296), [generationimpact.global](https://generationimpact.global/blog/espr-secondary-legislation-delegated-implementing-acts-quick-reference/) (search), acc. 2026-11-09.
- Ban scope = ESPR Annex VII products; operators must not destroy products supplied to them to circumvent the ban — [Textile School log, entry 19 Jul 2026](https://www.textileschool.com/compliance/changes/) (fetched), acc. 2026-11-09.

### Inferences
- Bangladeshi suppliers are not directly bound, but EU buyers will push return/overstock handling, donation documentation, and destruction-reason evidence into supplier contracts. Unsold stock that is returned to/bought back by a non-EU supplier is outside the EU ban, but anti-circumvention language (above) means EU buyers will be cautious.
- Disclosure data maps naturally to DPP-like product records (CN code + weight + quantity + disposition).

### Gaps
- Act numbers (EU) 2026/296 and (EU) 2026/2 not verified on EUR-Lex; "2026/2" looks inconsistent with a 9 Feb 2026 adoption date (OJ number 2 would be an early-January publication) — verify before citing in product UI.
- Exact first disclosure deadline/date in the standard format: sources say "February 2027" (Commission platform) vs "2 March 2027" (secondary) — unresolved.

## 3. Waste Framework Directive revision for textiles (EPR)

### Takeaway
ADOPTED: Directive (EU) 2025/1892 (dated 10 Sep 2025) creates mandatory EU-wide textile & footwear EPR. In force 16 Oct 2025; transposition by 17 Jun 2027; national EPR schemes must exist within 30 months of entry into force (~17 Apr 2028); micro-enterprises +12 months (~17 Apr 2029). Applies to all producers incl. non-EU e-commerce sellers; fees may be eco-modulated (durability, repairability, recyclability) and should account for (ultra-)fast fashion.

### Cited Findings
- EP press release (9 Sep 2025, ref. 20250905IPR30172): final approval under second reading; Member States must set up EPR schemes within 30 months of entry into force; producers making textiles available in the EU cover collection, sorting and recycling costs; rules apply to all producers "including e-commerce sellers", established in the EU or outside; micro-enterprises get one extra year; products: clothing and accessories, hats, footwear, blankets, bed and kitchen linen, curtains; Member States may include mattresses; Member States should consider ultra-fast and fast fashion practices when setting fees; transposition 20 months from entry into force — [European Parliament press release](https://www.europarl.europa.eu/news/en/press-room/20250905IPR30172/parliament-adopts-new-eu-rules-to-reduce-textile-and-food-waste) (fetched), acc. 2026-11-09.
- Directive date 10 Sep 2025; entry into force 16 Oct 2025; transposition 17 Jun 2027; textile EPR schemes mandatory April 2028; micro-enterprise deadline 17 Apr 2029; non-EU e-commerce sellers must register with national producer register (mechanics set nationally). Page "last verified 5 Oct 2026" (vendor page) — [digiproductpass.com WFD](https://www.digiproductpass.com/waste-framework-directive) (fetched), acc. 2026-11-09.
- OJ publication 26 Sep 2025; EPR schemes operational ~17 Apr 2028; eco-modulated fees; shipment controls on textile-waste exports — [EXPRA, 20 Dec 2025](https://expra.eu/2025/12/20/eu-publishes-revised-waste-framework-directive-mandatory-textile-epr-and-food-waste-targets-enter-into-force/) and others (search only; fetch returned empty), acc. 2026-11-09.
- Eco-modulation may be based on "durability, repairability and recyclability"; DPP data "could streamline reporting obligations"; earliest producer obligations possibly 2028 depending on national implementation — [CMS, 18 Sep 2025](https://cms.law/en/gbr/legal-updates/eu-parliament-approves-new-measures-on-textile-waste-what-manufacturers-retailers-and-e-commerce-sellers-need-to-know) (fetched), acc. 2026-11-09.
- Separately collected textiles are classified as waste under EU law; Waste Shipment Regulation (EU) 2024/1157 main provisions apply from 21 May 2026 (relevant to used-textile exports) — [Green Story](https://pages.greenstory.io/blog/directive-eu-2025-1892-amending-directive-2008-98-ec-on-waste-textile-epr-and-fo) (fetched), acc. 2026-11-09.
- Commission infringement/transposition tracking: Cullen International (Jun 2026) on transposition status of environmental directives — [Cullen International](https://www.cullen-international.com/news/2026/06/Transposition-status-of-key-EU-environmental-directives.html) (search), acc. 2026-11-09.

### Inferences
- The "producer" liable for fees is the entity first making the product available in each Member State (EU brand/importer, or a non-EU seller selling direct-to-consumer). A Bangladeshi factory selling FOB to an EU brand is not the producer; one selling DTC online into the EU would be.
- Reporting to PROs/registers will almost certainly require quantities placed on the market per Member State (units/weight) by product category, plus attributes needed for eco-modulation (fibre composition, durability/repairability info) — this is where DPP data reuse matters.

### Gaps
- Could not read the consolidated text (EUR-Lex fetch failed): exact Art. 22a-22d wording on producer registration, authorised-representative requirement for producers established outside a Member State, the precise reporting data set, and any Commission implementing acts on harmonised eco-modulation criteria/reporting formats were not verified in this session.
- National transposition status per Member State (France, Netherlands, Sweden, Hungary etc. already have/had national schemes) not checked.

## 4. Empowering Consumers for the Green Transition Directive (EU) 2024/825; Green Claims Directive

### Takeaway
ECGT is ADOPTED and APPLIES from 27 Sep 2026 (transposition deadline 27 Mar 2026, many Member States late). It bans generic environmental claims without recognised excellent performance, sustainability labels not based on certification schemes/public authorities, whole-product claims about one aspect, and offset-based "carbon neutral" product claims. The Green Claims Directive proposal was formally announced as WITHDRAWN in the Commission's 2027 work programme on 4 Nov 2026.

### Cited Findings
- Transposition deadline 27 Mar 2026; rules apply from 27 Sep 2026. On 28 May 2026 the Commission sent letters of formal notice for incomplete transposition to 20 Member States (Cullen says 19); ~10 Member States had completed transposition per Cullen (June 2026); examples: Belgium law published 4 Aug 2026, Luxembourg 9 Jun 2026 — [Jones Day / Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/consumer-trading-unfair-trading/1800382/eu-commission-pushes-for-transposition-of-the-empco-directive-on-environmental-claims); [Packaging Europe](https://packagingeurope.com/news/eu-commission-takes-action-against-20-member-states-over-green-claims-laws/14336.article); [Linklaters tracker](https://sustainablefutures.linklaters.com/post/102melt/eu-the-directive-on-empowering-consumers-for-the-green-transition-implementati); [Cullen International](https://www.cullen-international.com/news/2026/06/Transposition-status-of-key-EU-environmental-directives.html) (all search), acc. 2026-11-09.
- From 27 Sep 2026 prohibited: sustainability labels not based on a certification scheme or established by public authorities; generic environmental claims not substantiated by recognised excellent environmental performance; claims about the whole product/business when concerning only one aspect; carbon-neutral/reduced/positive product claims based on GHG offsetting; presenting legally required features as distinctive — [Textile School log, entry 27 Sep 2026](https://www.textileschool.com/compliance/changes/) (fetched; secondary), acc. 2026-11-09.
- Green Claims Directive: Commission announced official withdrawal with release of its 2027 work programme (article dated 4 Nov 2026); file "pending with co-legislators more than three years" and "unlikely to secure support in the Council within a reasonable timeframe"; file effectively paused in June 2025 after EPP request — [ESG Today, 4 Nov 2026](https://www.esgtoday.com/eu-commission-scraps-green-claims-directive/) (fetched), acc. 2026-11-09. Corroborated: [Net Zero](https://netzero.com.tr/regulations/european-commission-withdraws-proposed-green-claims-directive), [FoodTimes, 6 Nov 2026](https://www.foodtimes.eu/food-system/green-claims-directive-withdrawn-ecgt/) (search), acc. 2026-11-09. Some vendor guides dated late Oct 2026 still describe it as pending (pre-announcement) — [ecoclaim.eu](https://www.ecoclaim.eu/green-claims-directive) (search), acc. 2026-11-09.

### Inferences
- For a Bangladeshi exporter, ECGT matters via buyer marketing: claims like "eco-friendly", "sustainable cotton", "carbon neutral" on hangtags/labels supplied for EU retail must be backed by certification (e.g., third-party scheme) or specific, substantiated data. Supplier evidence (certificates, test reports, recycled-content proof) becomes buyer-critical.
- Commission work-programme withdrawals typically take legal effect on publication of the withdrawal in the OJ, so "announced 4 Nov 2026" may precede formal effect by weeks/months (procedural knowledge, not verified here).

### Gaps
- Primary text of Directive (EU) 2024/825 not fetched (EUR-Lex inaccessible); dates rely on multiple consistent secondary sources. Directive date given as 28 Feb 2024 (Commission annex per search) vs 2 Feb 2024 (Mondaq) — unresolved but immaterial.
- Exact OJ publication of the Green Claims withdrawal not checked.

## 5. Forced Labour Regulation (EU) 2024/3015

### Takeaway
ADOPTED; applies from 14 Dec 2027 to all products (incl. raw materials such as fibres) placed on, made available in, or exported from the EU market. Commission guidelines adopted 26 Jun 2026 (C(2026) 4386) and a Forced Labour Single Portal launched late June 2026; the risk-area/product database (legal deadline 14 Jun 2026) was still reported "under development" in July-August 2026.

### Cited Findings
- Guidelines published 26 Jun 2026; Regulation applies from 14 Dec 2027; covers all products and operators, including products already in warehouses on the application date; Commission leads investigations for risks outside the EU, national authorities inside; risk-based prioritisation (scale/severity, volume, share of component); preliminary phase 30 working days, investigations generally within nine months; decisions require withdrawal and disposal (>=30 working days to comply for non-perishables); authorities may request due-diligence info, supply-chain data and evidence, response typically 30-60 working days; due diligence not mandatory but documented efforts (OECD six-step) are valuable evidence; traceability systems, certification and worker monitoring may be considered; state-imposed forced labour covered; database still under development — [Baker McKenzie Global Import Blog, 24 Aug 2026](https://globalimportblog.bakermckenzie.com/2026/08/24/eu-publishes-guidelines-on-the-application-of-the-eu-forced-labour-regulation/) (fetched), acc. 2026-11-09.
- Guidelines document: [European Commission C(2026) 4386 final, Brussels 26.6.2026](https://webgate.ec.europa.eu/circabc-ewpp/d/d/workspace/SpacesStore/3b159733-6cdf-4f9e-8b30-80580c0679d8/download) (search), acc. 2026-11-09.
- Portal launch dated 26 Jun 2026 (Covington, Circulor) vs 30 Jun 2026 (Linklaters, LexisNexis); portal lacked the single information submission point and the risk database at launch; database's geographic/product risk info "still under development" — [Covington, Jul 2026](https://www.cov.com/en/news-and-insights/insights/2026/07/european-commission-publishes-forced-labour-regulation-guidelines-key-takeaways-for-companies); [STR Trade Report, Jul 2026](https://www.strtrade.com/trade-news-resources/str-trade-report/july/eu-forced-labor-ban-portal-is-open-and-the-clock-is-ticking); [Linklaters](https://sustainablefutures.linklaters.com/post/102n7tb/eu-forced-labour-regulation-commission-publishes-long-awaited-implementation-gui) (search), acc. 2026-11-09. Conflicting claim that the database was made available 26 Jun 2026 (Roedl) — [Roedl](https://www.roedl.com/en/insights/update-on-the-eu-forced-labour-regulation-the-european-commissions-guidelines-have-been-published/) (search); Dentons (13 Aug 2026) says database "not yet available" — [Dentons](https://www.dentons.com/en/insights/articles/2026/august/13/the-commission-publishes-new-guidelines-on-the-application-of-the-eu-forced-labour-regulation) (search), acc. 2026-11-09.
- Database legal content: indicative, non-exhaustive, evidence-based, regularly updated info on forced-labour risks in specific geographic areas or products/product groups, incl. state-imposed forced labour; must indicate specific economic sectors in specific geographic areas with reliable evidence of state-imposed forced labour; deadline 14 Jun 2026 — [Regulation (EU) 2024/3015, EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/ALL/?uri=CELEX%3A32024R3015) (search snippet of legal text), acc. 2026-11-09.

### Inferences
- Cotton supply chains are a core enforcement target in practice (state-imposed forced labour concerns historically documented for certain cotton-producing regions), so Bangladeshi exporters will be asked for fibre-origin traceability (ginner/spinner/country of cotton origin), certificates, and audit evidence. No source in this session names cotton or specific regions for the forthcoming database — treat as inference.
- Even a small tainted component taints the whole product; a DPP-style chain-of-custody record (bale -> yarn -> fabric -> garment) is the strongest defence in an information request.

### Gaps
- Whether the risk database has gone live between Aug and Nov 2026: unresolved (sources conflict; the official portal was not fetched).
- No source in this session specifically discussing cotton or Bangladesh under the FLR.

## 6. CSDDD and CSRD after the Omnibus I simplification

### Takeaway
ADOPTED: Omnibus I Directive (EU) 2026/470 (Council adoption 24 Feb 2026, OJ 26 Feb 2026, in force 18 Mar 2026). CSRD now only for >1,000 employees AND >EUR 450m turnover (from FY2027); CSDDD only for >5,000 employees AND >EUR 1.5bn turnover (non-EU: >EUR 1.5bn EU turnover), transposition 26 Jul 2028, application 26 Jul 2029. Supplier data requests to Bangladeshi factories will continue from in-scope buyers but are legally narrowed: a CSRD "value-chain cap" (VSME standard) for suppliers with <=1,000 employees, and CSDDD requests to partners <5,000 employees only as last resort, targeted and proportionate.

### Cited Findings
- Council adopted final text 24 Feb 2026; Parliament adopted 16 Dec 2025. CSRD: EU companies >1,000 employees and >EUR 450m net turnover (individual or consolidated); non-EU ultimate parents >EUR 450m EU turnover in each of last two FYs plus EU subsidiary/branch >EUR 200m; wave 3 listed SMEs removed; value-chain cap: in-scope companies cannot request information from "protected undertakings" (<=1,000 employees on average) beyond the VSME standard; reliance on self-declarations allowed; contrary contract terms not binding. CSDDD: >5,000 employees and >EUR 1.5bn worldwide turnover (EU); >EUR 1.5bn EU turnover (non-EU); franchising thresholds EUR 75m royalties / EUR 270m turnover; transposition 26 Jul 2028; compliance 26 Jul 2029; first reports FY starting on/after 1 Jan 2030; first Commission guidelines July 2027; scoping based on reasonably available information, prioritising direct business partners; requests to partners with <5,000 employees as last resort, targeted, reasonable, proportionate; termination-as-last-resort obligation removed; climate transition plan adoption obligation removed; EU-wide civil liability removed; max fine 3% of net worldwide turnover — [Clifford Chance, Feb 2026](https://www.cliffordchance.com/insights/resources/blogs/business-and-human-rights-insights/2026/02/omnibus-i-the-european-union-concludes-csddd-and-csrd-reforms.html) (fetched), acc. 2026-11-09.
- OJ publication 26 Feb 2026; entry into force 18 Mar 2026; CSRD transposition deadline 19 Mar 2027; CSDDD transposition 26 Jul 2028, compliance 26 Jul 2029, annual statement for FY from 1 Jan 2030; value-chain cap for value-chain companies with fewer than 1,000 employees — [Latham & Watkins, 27 Feb 2026](https://www.lw.com/en/insights/eu-sustainability-omnibus-published-in-the-official-journal) (fetched), acc. 2026-11-09.
- Directive number (EU) 2026/470; new CSRD scope applies from FY2027 — [Textile School log](https://www.textileschool.com/compliance/changes/) (fetched, lists "Omnibus I content Directive (2026/470)" and "stop-the-clock Directive (2025/794)"); search summaries of [Latham](https://www.lw.com/en/insights/eu-sustainability-omnibus-published-in-the-official-journal) / [Deutscher Nachhaltigkeitskodex](https://www.deutscher-nachhaltigkeitskodex.de/en/reporting-obligations/omnibus-regulation/) (search), acc. 2026-11-09.

### Inferences
- Large EU fashion groups (e.g., multi-billion-euro retailers) remain in CSDDD scope from July 2029, so Bangladeshi tier-1 suppliers will still receive due-diligence questionnaires — but buyers must now justify and target them.
- Many Bangladeshi garment factories employ well over 1,000 workers, so they may NOT be "protected undertakings" under the CSRD value-chain cap (cap applies only to <=1,000 employees); the CSDDD last-resort rule protects partners <5,000 employees, which covers most single factories but not the largest groups. (Inference from thresholds; no source applies this to Bangladesh.)
- Practically, a VSME-aligned supplier data pack (energy, GHG scope 1-2, workforce, H&S, wages) is the defensible "maximum" a CSRD-reporting buyer can demand from protected suppliers.

### Gaps
- Primary OJ text of Directive (EU) 2026/470 not fetched; VSME delegated act and revised ESRS adoption status (due ~4 and ~6 months after entry into force, i.e. ~Jul/Sep 2026) not verified.
- Whether the value-chain cap applies to non-EU suppliers in identical terms not confirmed from primary text.

## 7. EUDR (deforestation regulation): commodity scope and application date

### Takeaway
Cotton is NOT and never has been in EUDR scope (seven commodities: cattle, cocoa, coffee, palm oil, rubber, soy, wood). Cattle hides/skins/leather are being REMOVED from Annex I by a delegated act adopted 13 Jul 2026 (subject to 2-month scrutiny; secondary sources say effective ~18 Sep 2026). EUDR applies from 30 Dec 2026 (large/medium operators) and 30 Jun 2027 (other micro/small), after amendment Reg. (EU) 2025/2650; the Commission's 4 May 2026 review ruled out further postponement. Relevant residual exposure for apparel: wood-based packaging/paper, natural rubber components.

### Cited Findings
- Commission news 13 Jul 2026: delegated act updating Annex I removes "cattle hides, skins and leather", re-treaded tyres, soybeans for sowing, articles of vulcanised rubber, conveyor/transmission belts, aircraft and motor-vehicle seats; adds soluble coffee, certain palm-oil derivatives, frozen cattle tongues (subject from 30 Dec 2027); exempts samples, waste, used/second-hand products, packing material; act goes to EP/Council scrutiny first. "The Regulation will apply from 30 December 2026 for large and medium-sized operators and for micro and small operators already covered by the EU Timber Regulation ... and from 30 June 2027 for other micro and small operators." Information System reopened end of June 2026 — [European Commission DG ENV news, 13 Jul 2026](https://environment.ec.europa.eu/news/commission-updates-product-scope-and-tools-support-eudr-2026-07-13_en) (fetched), acc. 2026-11-09.
- Delegated act adopted 13 Jul 2026 with two-month scrutiny period; Commission docs C(2026) 4920 (delegated) and C(2026) 4732 (implementing); seven commodities, no cotton/textiles; standalone packaging (e.g., wooden pallets) in scope, packaging used only to support/protect another product not in scope — [Baker McKenzie Supply Chain Compliance, 16 Jul 2026](https://supplychaincompliance.bakermckenzie.com/2026/07/16/blog-post-eudr-european-commission-finalizes-the-eudr-product-scope-and-information-system-ahead-of-december-2026-application/) (fetched), acc. 2026-11-09.

### Inferences
- For a Bangladeshi woven/knit garment exporter, EUDR is near-irrelevant for the product itself. Leather goods/footwear makers in Bangladesh drop out of scope once the delegated act is in force. Remaining touchpoints: paper/cardboard hangtags/cartons sold as standalone products (not when only protecting goods), wooden hangers or pallets placed on the market, natural-rubber components if the HS code is listed.

## 8. Germany's LkSG in 2026

### Takeaway
The LkSG remains IN FORCE (due-diligence duties for companies with >=1,000 employees in Germany). The amendment bill abolishing the reporting duty retroactively (Drucksache 21/2474) had its first Bundestag reading on 16 Jan 2026; no source found confirms final passage or BGBl publication as of Nov 2026. BAFA stopped reviewing reports (1 Oct 2025) and suspended report submission (7 Nov 2025); fines are limited in practice to serious violations. A July 2026 coalition decision reportedly aims to raise the threshold to 5,000 employees and transpose CSDDD 1:1.

### Cited Findings
- Timeline: coalition agreement 14 Apr 2025 (replace LkSG with CSDDD-transposing law; drop reporting duty; sanctions only for serious human-rights violations); BMAS draft 29 Aug 2025; cabinet decision 3 Sep 2025 (reporting duty to lapse retroactively from 1 Jan 2023); Bundesrat opinion 17 Oct 2025; BAFA stopped reviewing reports 1 Oct 2025 (internal documentation duty remains, 7-year retention; fine offences reduced 13 -> 4, only serious violations); 7 Nov 2025 BAFA suspended review and submission of reports under ss. 12, 13 LkSG with immediate effect; page's latest dated entry 7 Nov 2025, planned schedule (2nd/3rd reading Dec 2025, Bundesrat Jan 2026) not confirmed as having happened — [IHK Nord Westfalen, Aktuelles zum LkSG](https://www.ihk.de/nordwestfalen/international/lieferketten/aktuelles-zum-lksg-6724308) (fetched), acc. 2026-11-09.

### Inferences
- German buyers (>=1,000 employees in Germany) still legally owe risk analysis, preventive measures, complaints mechanism and documentation covering direct suppliers (and indirect on substantiated knowledge), so Bangladeshi suppliers to German brands should still expect LkSG-driven code-of-conduct, audit and grievance-mechanism requests — but not public reporting.
