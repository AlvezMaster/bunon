# Bangladesh RMG Supply-Chain Reality and the Textile Traceability / DPP Competitive Landscape (as of 2026-11-09)

All URLs accessed 2026-11-09. Publication dates are given where the source shows them. Bangladesh's fiscal year (FY) runs July to June: FY25 is July 2024 to June 2025, and FY26 is July 2025 to June 2026.

## Q1. Bangladesh RMG facts: export value, share of exports, EU share, factories, workers, women (dossier check)

### Takeaway
The latest full-year EPB figures put RMG exports at US$39.34B in FY25 (81.5% of $48.28B total exports) and $38.70B in FY26 (down 1.64%). The EU took 50.1% in FY25 and 49.25% in FY26. The team dossier's "$47B / 84% / 4.4M workers / 4,000+ factories" is mostly wrong or inflated. $47B is close to *total* merchandise exports (calendar 2025: $47.74B), not RMG. The 84% figure appears in some press but EPB arithmetic gives about 81 to 82%. The best registry counts are about 3.0 to 3.3M workers and about 3,555 export-oriented factories.

### Cited Findings
**Export value and share (EPB)**
- FY25 (July 2024 to June 2025): RMG exports were US$39.34B, up 8.84% year on year, and **81.49% of total export earnings**, per EPB data — [Financial Express, "Bangladesh's RMG exports show moderate growth, EU remains key market"](https://thefinancialexpress.com.bd/trade/bangladeshs-rmg-exports-show-moderate-growth-eu-remains-key-market). Other outlets give $39.35B ([Apparel Resources](https://apparelresources.com/business-news/trade/bangladeshs-rmg-exports-grow-8-84-fy-25/)) and $39.3B ([Fibre2Fashion](https://www.fibre2fashion.com/news/apparel-news/bangladesh-s-rmg-exports-up-8-8-to-39-3-bn-in-fy25-303779-newsdetails.htm)).
- FY25 total merchandise exports were US$48.28B — [Financial Express, "Exports fetch $48.28b in FY25"](https://thefinancialexpress.com.bd/trade/exports-fetch-4828b-in-fy25). JFBD gives total-export growth of 8.58% — [JFBD](https://jfbd.com/major-exports-imports/).
- FY26 (July 2025 to June 2026): RMG exports were **US$38.70B, down 1.64%** from $39.34B. Knitwear fell 2.53% and woven fell 0.61%. Source is EPB via UNB, published 7 July 2026 — [Financial Express, "EU share erodes as Bangladesh RMG exports slip 1.64pc to $38.70b in FY26"](https://thefinancialexpress.com.bd/home/eu-share-erodes-as-bangladesh-rmg-exports-slip-164pc-to-3870b-in-fy26). The FY26 total merchandise export figure was not in this article.
- FY26 first seven months (July to January): RMG earned $22.98B, about 81% of total exports, while total exports fell 1.93% to $28.41B — [Apparel Resources](https://apparelresources.com/business-news/trade/bangladeshs-rmg-exports-fall-2-43-us-22-98-billion-july-january-fy-26).
- **Calendar 2025:** total merchandise exports were **US$47.74B, down "nearly 5%"** from 2024, per EPB monthly data. The article says garments make up "more than 84%" of export earnings. July to December 2025 garment exports were $19.36B, down 2.63%. Published 4 January 2026 — [The Daily Star, "Exports fell nearly 5% in 2025"](https://www.thedailystar.net/business/news/exports-fell-nearly-5-2025-4073381).
- December 2025 exports were about $3.96B (down 14.25% year on year, the fifth straight monthly decline), with apparel at $3.23B (down 14.23%) — [The Daily Star](https://online-d11.thedailystar.net/business/news/exports-fall-fifth-straight-month-396b-december-4072956).

**Market split**
- FY25: the EU took **$19.71B, or 50.10%** of RMG exports. The US took $7.54B (19.18%). Non-traditional markets took $6.44B (16.36%) — [Financial Express](https://thefinancialexpress.com.bd/trade/bangladeshs-rmg-exports-show-moderate-growth-eu-remains-key-market).
- FY26: the EU took **$19.06B (down 3.31%), or 49.25%**. The US took $7.74B (20.01%), the UK $4.39B (11.34%), Canada $1.34B (3.47%) and non-traditional markets $6.16B (15.93%) — [Financial Express, 7 July 2026](https://thefinancialexpress.com.bd/home/eu-share-erodes-as-bangladesh-rmg-exports-slip-164pc-to-3870b-in-fy26).
- On the EU import side (Eurostat, calendar year), Bangladesh's apparel exports to the EU were €18.06B for January to November 2025, against €16.78B for all of 2024. Bangladesh is the EU's second-largest apparel supplier, with a 21.57% share in 2025. This is a secondary report citing Eurostat — [B Mirror](https://bmirror.net/rmg-exports-to-eu-remain-resilient-despite-global-demand-slump/).
- A Bangladesh Today and Apparel Resources piece says "approximately 60 per cent" of garment exports go to the EU — [Apparel Resources](https://apparelresources.com/business-news/sustainability/bangladesh-garment-industry-pilot-blockchain-enabled-digital-product-passport-system/). This conflicts with EPB's roughly 50%. See Inferences.

**Factories**
- Mapped in Bangladesh (MiB), an open factory registry, counted **3,555 export-oriented RMG factories as of December 2024**. Of these, 2,720 were affiliated with BGMEA or BKMEA and 836 were not (these add to 3,556, so the source is off by one) — [Financial Express, "RMG industry lacks full database of workers", 16 Feb 2025](https://thefinancialexpress.com.bd/trade/rmg-industry-lacks-full-database-of-workers).
- BGMEA active member factories: 1,806 after about 690 were removed in an early-2025 membership audit — [Apparel Resources](https://apparelresources.com/business-news/trade/around-690-bgmea-members-removed-active-factory-list-amid-membership-renewal-process/). There were 2,107 in March 2025 (1,769 in Dhaka and 338 in Chattogram) — [bdnews24](https://bdnews24.com/business/d8c87eee78fa). A UNB Eid wage report counted 2,134 operational factories, of which 2,133 were active (year not clearly stated) — [UNB](https://unb.com.bd/category/Business/over-94-rmg-factories-pay-eid-bonuses-99-clear-apr-wages-bgmea/186795).
- One report says 256 factories closed and 323 opened over about three years and two months — [search summary of bdnews24 and TBS](https://www.tbsnews.net/node/1532431). A second report puts closures at 457 factories in two years, citing energy and order deficits — [Dhaka Tribune](https://www.dhakatribune.com/business/418095/energy-work-order-deficits-shut-457-factories).

**Workers and women**
- MiB counted **about 3.04 million workers, 57.7% of them women** (December 2024 data). The Financial Express calls the commonly cited 4.0 million workers "mythical" and the 80% female share a myth — [Financial Express, 16 Feb 2025](https://thefinancialexpress.com.bd/trade/rmg-industry-lacks-full-database-of-workers).
- BGMEA's biometric data as of June 2024 recorded **3,317,397 workers, 52.28% of them women** (about 1.73M) — [Fibre2Fashion, "55-57% of RMG workers in Bangladesh women"](https://www.fibre2fashion.com/news/manufacturing-news/55-57-of-rmg-workers-in-bangladesh-women-data-from-trade-bodies-296228-newsdetails.htm).
- The female share was about 80% in 1980 to 1994 (BGMEA data), about 70% in 2005, and about 53% in 2021 and 2023 (ETI, GIZ, BRAC and BIDS studies). Women hold under 15% of supervisory roles and under 5% of managerial roles (2022). Published 25 July 2026 — [The Daily Star, "Women built the RMG might, now they are losing ground"](https://www.thedailystar.net/business/news/women-built-the-rmg-might-now-they-are-losing-ground-4231581).
- The ETI, GIZ and BRAC study put the female share at 53.65% in 2021 — [ETI/GIZ study PDF](https://ethicaltrade.org/sites/default/files/shared_resources/ETI_GIZ%20Study%20on%20Women%20Workers.pdf).

### Inferences
- **Dossier check.**
  - **"$47B":** this matches *total* merchandise exports (calendar 2025: $47.74B; FY25: $48.28B), not RMG. RMG was $39.3B in FY25 and $38.7B in FY26. Use about $39B for RMG.
  - **"84% of exports":** The Daily Star printed "more than 84%" (January 2026). EPB-derived figures give 81.49% for FY25 and about 81% for FY26 to date. Cite "about 81 to 82% (EPB, FY25)". If the team keeps 84%, it should name the source and note that it likely uses a broader textile definition (this definition question is unverified).
  - **"4.4M workers":** no source found supports it. Registry and biometric data give 3.04M (MiB, December 2024) to 3.32M (BGMEA, June 2024). The industry's own folk figure is 4.0M.
  - **"4,000+ factories":** no source found supports it for export-oriented factories. MiB counts 3,555. BGMEA has about 2,100 active members.
- The "60% of exports go to the EU" claim probably counts EU plus UK (50.1% + about 11% ≈ 61% in FY25). The EU27 alone is about 49 to 50%. A DPP pitch should say "about half of RMG exports (≈$19B) go to the EU27, plus about 11% to the UK".
- The FY26 decline (−1.64%) and the EU share erosion strengthen the case that compliance-readiness tooling is a way to keep EU orders. That is an argument, not a measured effect.

### Gaps
- No EPB figure for calendar-2025 *RMG* exports was found; only total exports ($47.74B) and the July to December half are available.
- No FY26 total merchandise export figure (and so no FY26 RMG share) was found.
- No ILO or World Bank 2025–26 worker figure was retrieved. The 2024 BGMEA and MiB data are the most recent primary counts found.

## Q2. Certification fraud, scope certificates vs transaction certificates, and how third parties verify GOTS, Textile Exchange and OEKO-TEX claims

### Takeaway
Documented fraud since 2020 includes forged raw-cotton transaction certificates (TCs) with QR codes pointing to a cloned government website, and repeated suspensions of certification bodies. Only a transaction certificate proves a specific shipment. A scope certificate only proves a site is capable of producing certified goods. Public databases verify scope certificates and labels well, but until recently there was no central lookup for TCs. Digital TCs are only now being piloted, including in Bangladesh.

### Cited Findings
**Documented fraud cases**
- **GOTS, November 2020:** GOTS and its accreditation body IOAS found about **20,000 tonnes of fake organic cotton** in India. Fraudsters made fake raw-cotton TCs from templates of APEDA (India's agricultural export authority). The QR codes on the forged certificates led to a **cloned APEDA website**. GOTS told certification bodies (CBs) to cancel the upstream certificates and said it would itself check future raw-cotton TCs entering its supply chain — [GOTS press release](https://global-standard.org/news/gots-press-release-gots-detects-evidence-of-organic-cotton-fraud-in-india); [Just Style](https://www.just-style.com/news/fake-organic-cotton-found-by-gots-in-india/); [Textile Network, dated 02/11/2020](https://textile-network.com/en/Fasern-Garne/GOTS-findet-Beweise).
- Textile Exchange said it banned the companies involved and terminated the licensing contract of one certification body. It also noted that standard-setters cannot grant or withdraw site certification directly; that power sits with the CBs — [Just Style](https://www.just-style.com/news/fake-organic-cotton-found-by-gots-in-india/).
- GOTS banned 5 Odisha-based cotton companies for forged TCs whose QR codes led to a spoof website (date not shown) — [Apparel Resources](https://apparelresources.com/business-news/trade/gots-bans-5-indian-cotton-companies-faking-documents).
- **New York Times, February 2022**, "That Organic Cotton T-Shirt May Not Be as Organic as You Think". Indian sources quoted in it estimated that one-half to four-fifths of organic cotton sold from India is not genuine. It noted that neither GOTS nor Textile Exchange inspects farms itself and that **"there is no central database to look up the transaction numbers"** to check whether a TC was already used. It also said Textile Exchange reported 48% growth in Indian organic cotton output in one year. These details come from secondary coverage — [ASI Central, 15 Feb 2022](https://members.asicentral.com/news/web-exclusive/february-2022/report-not-all-organic-cotton-is-actually-organic/); [NZ Herald syndication](https://nzherald.co.nz/lifestyle/that-organic-cotton-t-shirt-may-not-be-as-organic-as-you-think/FDCEHOMBIM6YQNR4F4XNTKV7NQ); [Naked Capitalism](https://nakedcapitalism.com/2022/02/nyt-recognizes-that-organic-cotton-t-shirt-may-not-be-as-organic-as-you-think.html).
- Ecotextile's April 2022 feature, "Breaking point for organic cotton integrity" — [Ecotextile](https://ecotextile.com/2022041229223/features/breaking-point-for-organic-cotton-integrity.html).
- Textile Exchange's 2022 Organic Cotton Market Report reportedly rated its confidence in India data as *low* and noted that APEDA figures mixed organic and in-conversion cotton. This is a secondary report — [Texfash](https://texfash.com/special/global-confidence-in-organic-cotton-dips-reason-the-great-indian-organic-cotton-scam).
- **Control Union India, 2023:** IOAS suspended CU India's organic textile accreditation (GOTS and Textile Exchange scopes) in March 2023. GOTS presented this as evidence its checks work — [GOTS statement](https://global-standard.org/news/gots-statement-control-union-india-2023). The suspension was later lifted for Textile Exchange work — [Just Style](https://www.just-style.com/news/suspension-lifted-on-indian-organic-cotton-certification-body).
- **GSCI** was suspended from all Textile Exchange standards from 11 August 2023. **AsureQuality** was suspended for Textile Exchange animal-fibre farm scope from 29 March 2023 — [Just Style](https://www.just-style.com/news/asurequality-suspended-as-textile-exchange-certifier-of-animal-fibres); [search summary of Just Style and Texdata](https://texdata.com/news/Sustainability/20820.html).
- **Intertek India, 2024:** Intertek was suspended for the Organic Content Standard in India. Textile Exchange's FAQ says previously issued TCs stay valid unless the CB or Textile Exchange says otherwise — [Textile Exchange Intertek Suspension FAQ, August 2024](https://textileexchange.org/app/uploads/2024/08/Intertek-Suspension-FAQ.pdf).
- A January 2025 PR-wire "exposé" alleges bribery and fraud involving Intertek, GOTS and Textile Exchange. It is self-published and unverified — [Mammoth Times / GlobePRWire](https://business.mammothtimes.com/mammothtimes/article/globeprwire-2025-1-25-exposed-the-truth-behind-organic-cotton-and-key-players-revealed).

**Scope certificate vs transaction certificate**
- A **scope certificate (SC)** certifies an *operator/site*. It confirms the holder can process or trade the listed product categories to the standard, and the holder appears in the GOTS Certified Suppliers Database — [GOTS certification page](https://global-standard.org/certification-and-labelling/certification).
- A **transaction certificate (TC)** is issued by the *seller's* CB for a **specific shipment**. It lists the products, quantities, shipment details and the buyer. **The TC is the document that proves a given shipment is certified** — [GOTS TC procedure v4.0](https://global-standard.org/images/resource-library/documents/certificate-policies-and-templates/Procedure%20%20Template%20For%20Issuance%20Of%20Transaction%20Certificates%20-%20GOTS%20Version%204.0.signed.pdf); [Textile Exchange ASR-104 Policy for Transaction Certificates v2.2](https://textileexchange.org/app/uploads/2021/02/ASR-104-V2.2-Policy-for-Transaction-Certificates.pdf); [Retraced help center](https://help.retraced.com/en/articles/8508501-the-function-of-scope-and-transaction-certificates).
- A TC is valid only if the seller's SC, held with the *same CB*, was valid on the shipment date. Suspended, withdrawn or expired SCs invalidate it. Every product on the TC must be listed on the SC. GOTS and Textile Exchange have harmonised their TC policy and templates — [GOTS TC procedure v4.0](https://global-standard.org/images/resource-library/documents/certificate-policies-and-templates/Procedure%20%20Template%20for%20Issuance%20of%20Transaction%20Certificates%20-%20GOTS_%204.0_Signed.pdf); [Delogue explainer](https://www.delogue.com/en/blog/understanding-scope-transaction-certificates).

**Verification tools**
- **GOTS:** the public Certified Suppliers Database lists SC holders. Users can search by location, field of operation, product category or licence number, and CBs update the entries. The FAQ cautions that a listing does not prove every product is GOTS certified, that subcontractors are not displayed, and that buyers should obtain a TC per shipment. A product-level traceability database has been in development since 2021 — [GOTS Find Suppliers](https://global-standard.org/find-suppliers-shops-and-inputs); [GOTS Q&A](https://global-standard.org/q-and-a).
- **Textile Exchange:** it keeps a certified-organisations / OCS-certified suppliers lookup and a public **banned organisations list**. Textile Exchange has itself said the database is still being refined and some valid certificates may be missing — [Textile Exchange "Where can I source organic cotton?"](https://textileexchange.org/?p=26123); [WFX blog summary](https://worldfashionexchange.com/blog/?p=26444).
- **OEKO-TEX:** **Label Check** is a free public portal that resolves a certificate or label number, e.g. `oeko-tex.com/v/<number>` — [OEKO-TEX Label Check example](https://www.oeko-tex.com/v/22CX00066). OEKO-TEX also offers an **API** that returns certificate validity, issue and expiry dates and product class for STANDARD 100, so companies can build it into their own systems (announced 2014) — [Specialty Fabrics Review, "Validating OEKO-TEX", 2014](https://specialtyfabricsreview.com/2014/08/01/validating-oeko-tex/); [Texdata](https://texdata.com/news/Textiles&Apparel-Garment/7891.Simple-validation-of-OEKO-TEX(r)-certificates-with-newly-developed-API.html). OEKO-TEX also runs a "CertLink" project — [OEKO-TEX CertLink](https://oeko-tex.com/en/certlink-project). One verification guide warns that supplier-emailed "verification" links can be phishing and advises going to the official site directly — [Shanghai Garment guide](https://shanghaigarment.com/?p=54387).
- **Digital TCs (new):** in February 2026 OEKO-TEX chose **TextileGenesis** to digitise organic-cotton TCs. A pilot ran from **March to August 2025 in India and Bangladesh** with 11 supply-chain actors, 24 certificates and 19 certified transactions — [FashionUnited, 12 Feb 2026](https://fashionunited.uk/press/business/oeko-tex-r-chooses-textilegenesis-to-advance-digital-traceability-for-organic-cotton/2026021286269); [Textile Network, 25 Feb 2026](https://textile-network.de/en/Technical-Textiles/Technologien/Digital-Traceability-of-Organic-Cotton).
- Earlier, Textile Exchange and TextileGenesis piloted **"eTrackit"**, an electronic alternative to PDF TCs for the Global Recycled Standard and Recycled Claim Standard. Seven brands took part, including H&M Group and Inditex. The collaboration began in October 2019 — [Textile Exchange webinar deck, September 2020](https://textileexchange.org/app/uploads/2021/05/Webinar-Textile-Exchange-and-TextileGenesis-Collaboration-September-2-2020.pdf).

### Inferences
- Bunon's "verify supplier certificates" step needs to work at the **TC** level: check that the TC number exists, matches the shipment, the seller's SC was valid on the shipment date with the same CB, the product is in the SC's scope, the TC hasn't been used before, and the issuing CB wasn't suspended. An SC lookup alone is the weakness the 2020 fraud exploited. Checking the domain behind a QR code matters too, since the forged TCs linked to a cloned website.
- Standard-setters are moving TCs into their own or partner platforms (OEKO-TEX + TextileGenesis; Textile Exchange eTrackit). A supplier-side tool will probably need to *ingest* these as authoritative sources rather than compete with them.
- OEKO-TEX is the only scheme with a documented public validation API among the three. For GOTS and Textile Exchange, programmatic verification would likely mean scraping public databases or making a partnership agreement. This is an inference; no public API was found.

### Gaps
- No public API was confirmed for the GOTS database or the Textile Exchange certified-organisations database.
- It was not confirmed whether GOTS now issues TCs through a central online system that supports third-party lookup by TC number.
- The current status (2026) of the OEKO-TEX API's access terms is not confirmed.
- No documented TC fraud case specifically involving Bangladeshi exporters was found.

## Q3. Fibre-origin verification beyond documents (isotopic, DNA and luminescent markers)

### Takeaway
Two families exist. **Forensic origin testing** (Oritain, Eurofins and GenuTrace isotope analysis) checks *where* the fibre grew without adding anything to it. **Added tracers** (Haelixa and Applied DNA markers, FibreTrace luminescent pigment) are applied at the gin or spinner and checked downstream. Brands mainly use them for forced-labour (UFLPA/Xinjiang) risk and for premium-fibre claims, usually as spot tests rather than for every shipment.

### Cited Findings
- **Oritain** measures isotope ratios and trace elements with statistical models to determine origin. Its own framing is that DNA testing says *what* a product is, while isotopes say *where* it is from — [Oritain cotton traceability blog](https://oritain.com/resources/blog/cotton-traceability). Oritain says its cotton testing is accepted as evidence for UFLPA compliance under DHS and CBP guidance (vendor claim) — [Oritain cotton & fashion](https://oritain.com/industries/cotton-fashion).
- Oritain's 2026 Global Supply Chain Intelligence Report (14 May 2026) covered 1,000 garments a year over five years across 40 brands. It found **90% of brands analysed in 2025 had at least one "prohibited cotton" result, up from 64% in 2024**. This is self-reported, and no regions are named in the release — [PR Newswire Asia](https://en.prnasia.com/releases/apac/oritain-launches-landmark-2026-global-supply-chain-intelligence-report-533057.shtml).
- The U.S. Cotton Trust Protocol launched forensic verification with Oritain, with selected suppliers asked for samples from May 2026. Next Level Apparel expanded its Oritain "Gold" membership in 2026 — [search summary; Impressions](https://impressionsmagazine.com/news/next-level-apparel-oritain-gold-membership/169124/).
- **Eurofins Origin ID** offers cotton stable-isotope origin testing with a turnaround of about 15 working days (May 2026) — [Eurofins](https://sustainabilityservices.eurofins.com/news/cotton-origin-verification-strengthening-supply-chain-traceability/).
- **Haelixa** is an ETH Zürich spin-off with a plant-based DNA marker sprayed as a liquid onto raw material, at the ginning stage in the CottonConnect project — [Just Style](https://www.just-style.com/news/cottonconnect-haelixa-partner-to-trace-cotton-digitally-from-seed-to-garment). New Focus Textiles adopted it for GRS recycled cotton — [Just Style](https://www.just-style.com/newsletters/new-focus-textile-adopts-haelixa-dna-traceability-tech). Customer claims naming C&A and Soorty come from a low-reliability AI-generated profile and are unverified — [geo.sig.ai](https://geo.sig.ai/brands/haelixa.md).
- **Applied DNA Sciences (CertainT / SigNature)** embeds synthetic DNA "molecular barcodes" in cotton and recycled polyester. Named manufacturers include Himatsingka, Pratibha Syntex, Ganesha Ecosphere (rPET) and A&E Gütermann (thread) — [Apparel Resources](https://apparelresources.com/business-news/sustainability/dna-marking-technology-enhancing-traceability-combating-textile-fraud/). The brand names in the same results (Lacoste, H&M, Bestseller, Kering) were not verified against primary announcements.
- **FibreTrace** adds a luminescent pigment to cotton at the gin, which can be scanned on site anywhere downstream. In June 2024 it partnered with **Target and Cargill** to cover 50,000 t of US and Brazilian cotton in year one, with products expected on shelves in early 2025. It says it works with 15 brands publicly — [FashionUnited, June 2024](https://fashionunited.uk/news/business/fibretrace-partners-with-target-and-cargill-to-trace-cotton/2024060675945); [Retail Dive](https://www.retaildive.com/news/target-fibretrace-and-cargill-partner-to-verify-raw-cotton/717846). Other partners include Thai Acrylic Fibre — [Just Style](https://www.just-style.com/news/thai-acrylic-fibre-incorporates-fibretrace-technology-for-end-to-end-fashion-traceability/) — and an ARISE IIP pilot in Benin — [Just Style](https://www.just-style.com/news/fibretrace-to-create-traceable-cotton-supply-chain-in-africa/). SGS works with FibreTrace through its "Fiber Integrity Protocol" — [Specialty Fabrics Review, January 2024](https://specialtyfabricsreview.com/2024/01/01/transparency-tech-solutions-for-materials-tracking/).
- One supplier-written guide notes that a certificate check confirms the paper, not the goods. Linking the two needs lot-level traceability that ties fabric lot numbers to certificate numbers — [Shanghai Garment](https://shanghaigarment.com/?p=54387).

### Inferences
- For Bunon, forensic and tracer tests are best treated as *evidence attachments* to a provenance record (a lab report ID, sample ID and result hash) rather than built in-house. Bangladesh imports most of its fibre and yarn (mainly from China and India, per the BGMEA–AWARE coverage in Q4), so origin risk sits upstream of the Bangladeshi exporter.
- Isotope testing takes days to weeks (Eurofins: about 15 working days) and is sample-based. It fits risk-triggered audits, not per-shipment gating.

### Gaps
- No public pricing was found for Oritain, Haelixa, Applied DNA or FibreTrace tests.
- No 2025–26 FibreTrace announcements were found.
- No case of a Bangladeshi exporter using these tests was found.

## Q4. Competitive landscape: traceability and DPP vendors, and Bangladesh-focused initiatives

### Takeaway
The established players (TrusTrace, TextileGenesis/Lectra, Retraced, Fairly Made, Carbonfact, Renoon, EON, Avery Dennison atma.io, Kezzler, Arianee) are almost all **sold to brands**, with suppliers onboarded as free or low-touch data contributors. None publishes prices. Several Bangladesh-specific, **supplier-side** efforts appeared in 2025–26. BGMEA signed MoUs with DigiProd Pass (May 2025, 24-month blockchain DPP pilot) and with AWARE (May 2026, "built for producers", factories keep their data). BGMEA also runs an ESG data platform with 300+ factories, and factories such as Urmi, DBL and Square already have DPP systems. Bunon's "supplier-side compliance engine" positioning is therefore contested inside Bangladesh, not empty.

### Cited Findings
**Brand-side DPP and traceability platforms**
- **TrusTrace** (traceability, supplier engagement, material-level data, DPP compliance). It uses OCR to extract data from certificates that suppliers upload during traceability requests — [TrusTrace DPP Buying Guide](https://trustrace.com/knowledge-hub/the-dpp-technology-buying-guide-with-checklist); [TrusTrace DPP solution](https://trustrace.com/solutions/digital-product-passport-dpp-compliance). Gartner named it a "Market Shaper" in its Emerging Market Quadrant for DPP, Established Vendors (July 2026) — [TrusTrace newsroom](https://trustrace.com/newsroom/trustrace-named-a-market-shaper-in-gartner-emerging-market-quadrant-for-digital-product-passport). ### Inferences
- **Buyer split:** every global vendor above is bought by brands, which roll suppliers into their own portal. That is why Urmi juggles 20+ portals. The open space is a *supplier-owned* single source of truth that pushes data into many brand portals ("enter once, publish many"). AWARE and DigiProd already pitch this to BGMEA, so Bunon's differentiators would need to be certificate-level verification (TC validation and fraud checks), working offline or on-premises, and interoperability with TextileGenesis, TrusTrace and other brand platforms rather than replacing them.
- BGMEA has endorsed at least two external DPP vendors (DigiProd, AWARE) plus its own ESG platform. Any new entrant would probably need BGMEA alignment or an integration story to get factory adoption.
- No vendor publishes pricing. Expect enterprise contracts negotiated by sales, paid by brands. Supplier-side willingness to pay is unproven (see Q5).

## Q5. Who pays for DPP and traceability, Bangladesh supplier pain points, and DPP pilots with Bangladeshi suppliers

### Takeaway
Brands buy the platforms, but Bangladeshi factories carry the data-entry and compliance cost across many fragmented brand formats. BGMEA is publicly asking for a "shared responsibility" model where brands fund the setup. The main pain points are analog upstream mills, no lot-level tracking, SME capital and connectivity limits, duplicate reporting and audit fatigue. Several DPP pilots now include Bangladeshi suppliers: OEKO-TEX × TextileGenesis (2025), BGMEA × DigiProd (2025–27), BGMEA × AWARE (2026), and Circular Textiles Bangladesh (October 2026).

### Cited Findings
- BGMEA vice-president Vidiya Amrit Khan said European buyers impose **fragmented reporting formats while local factories bear the financial burden**. BGMEA wants a **"shared responsibility" model in which brands help fund system setup**. BKMEA called for a centralised, interoperable national data framework — [Financial Express, 5 October 2026](https://thefinancialexpress.com.bd/trade/project-launched-to-drive-supply-chain-traceability-decarbonisation-in-rmg-sector).
- The same event reported that many backward-linkage knitting, spinning and dyeing units **still run analog systems** and lack the infrastructure and trained staff to track lot-to-lot yarn and fabric. IoT and flow-meter upgrades need capital that SMEs can't afford without targeted financing. There is a large gap between big groups and SMEs, and brand-specific reporting creates redundancy. Team Group's Mohammad Monower Hossain said suppliers must verify four data areas: **product composition, facility environmental metrics, social evidence and chain-of-custody traceability** — [Financial Express, 5 October 2026](https://thefinancialexpress.com.bd/trade/project-launched-to-drive-supply-chain-traceability-decarbonisation-in-rmg-sector).
- A DPP readiness roundtable cited siloed data, limited upstream traceability, insufficient data granularity and uneven technology readiness across tiers. Participants called for a unified data system to reduce duplication and **audit fatigue** — [Financial Express, "DPP readiness gaps pose challenge for RMG sector"](https://thefinancialexpress.com.bd/trade/dpp-readiness-gaps-pose-challenge-for-rmg-sector-roundtable).
- SMEs "often lack access to digital infrastructure, reliable connectivity and financing" — [Apparel Resources, "Bangladesh gears up for DPP era"](https://apparelresources.com/business-news/sustainability/bangladesh-gears-digital-product-passport-era/). An OVS sustainability manager said: "Most of our suppliers don't even know what traceability means" — [search summary of Financial Express reporting](https://thefinancialexpress.com.bd/national/awareness-gaps-regulatory-hurdles-threaten-traceability-of-export-goods-experts).
- A study reported Bangladesh's export supply chain has "66 info gaps" — [Financial Express](https://thefinancialexpress.com.bd/home/bangladeshs-export-supply-chain-riddled-with-66-info-gaps). Details were not retrieved.
- Smaller garment makers may need hardware, software and data-management upgrades for DPPs, and DigiProd plans pilots and training for small businesses — [Apparel Resources](https://apparelresources.com/business-news/sustainability/bangladesh-garment-industry-pilot-blockchain-enabled-digital-product-passport-system/).
- Under the BGMEA–AWARE MoU, factories keep ownership of and control over who sees their data — [Just Style, 12 May 2026](https://www.just-style.com/news/bgmea-aware-dpp-bangaldesh-garment/).
- Vendor pricing models are brand-paid subscriptions with supplier-portal access (Retraced) — [Net Zero Compare](https://netzerocompare.com/software/retraced). TrusTrace extracts data from certificates that suppliers upload — [TrusTrace](https://trustrace.com/knowledge-hub/the-dpp-technology-buying-guide-with-checklist).
- **Pilots involving Bangladeshi suppliers:**
  - OEKO-TEX × TextileGenesis digital organic-cotton TCs, March to August 2025 in **India and Bangladesh** — [FashionUnited](https://fashionunited.uk/press/business/oeko-tex-r-chooses-textilegenesis-to-advance-digital-traceability-for-organic-cotton/2026021286269).

### Inferences
- The hackathon value proposition that fits best is "one supplier record, many brand outputs": cut the 20+ portal problem, run offline-tolerant or on-premises for weak connectivity, and give SMEs a cheap or free tier, possibly paid by brands, BGMEA or donors such as RVO and GIZ. - Suppliers resist paying per brand. Monetisation is more plausible through BGMEA bulk licensing, donor programmes, or brands paying to receive verified supplier data.
- Data ownership is a stated priority for BGMEA, given the explicit AWARE clause. Bunon's "factory controls access" design should be stated plainly in the pitch.

## Q6. Data carriers in apparel: GS1 Digital Link QR, RFID, NFC, woven labels

### Takeaway
In practice: **QR codes (increasingly GS1 Digital Link, GTIN plus serial) printed on care labels or hangtags** are the default consumer-facing DPP carrier. **UHF RAIN RFID** is widely used for retail inventory and logistics and can encode the same identifier. **NFC** is mostly a luxury and authentication play. GS1 says the identifiers and carriers already exist and the hard part is the data. Whether the EU will mandate a specific carrier was not confirmed.

### Cited Findings
- GS1 Digital Link encodes GS1 identifiers (e.g. GTIN plus serial) as a web URI that can be printed as a QR code or carried on NFC or RFID. Scanning hits a resolver that returns DPP data, for example as JSON. A printed QR on a care label or hangtag is "most practical" for many apparel items, and UHF EPC RFID suits logistics-heavy SKUs and can map to the same identifier. This is vendor and industry commentary — [Cleverence](https://www.cleverence.com/articles/business-blogs/strategic-pivot-toward-digital-product-passports-for-textiles-4827/).
- GS1 UK says identifiers, data carriers such as QR codes and the infrastructure linking products to digital information "already exist and are used across many apparel businesses", and the harder problem is the data itself — [GS1 UK, "DPP in apparel: why industry alignment matters now"](https://gs1uk.org/insights/news/Digital-Product-Passport-in-apparel-why-industry-alignment-matters-now). GS1 UK on DPP and textile recycling — [GS1 UK](https://www.gs1uk.org/insights/news/Digital-Product-Passports-unlocking-a-revolution-in-textile-recycling). GS1 in Europe's apparel and textile DPP briefing (2023) — [GS1 EU PDF](https://gs1.eu/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/The-DPP-ApparelTextile-Industry-Briefing-Document.pdf). There is also a GS1 Sweden textile DPP pilot — [GS1 Sweden](https://gs1.se/en/case/digital-product-passport-pilot-within-textiles/).
- Checkpoint and the RAIN Alliance say their RFID labels, middleware and DPP platform are built on RAIN RFID plus GS1 Digital Link. This is a vendor claim — [RAIN Alliance](https://therainalliance.org/?p=50031).
- Avery Dennison's ReadyDPP (July 2026) bundles digital IDs, labels and RFID hardware with DPP software — [RFID Journal](https://www.rfidjournal.com/?p=225179).
- Retraced QR codes are used on care labels and hangtags (KnowledgeCotton), and Carbonfact DPP QR codes resolve to a stable product URL — [FashionUnited, June 2026](https://fashionunited.com/news/business/how-to-do-it-brands-talk-about-dpp-implementation/2026061572935); [Carbonfact](https://www.carbonfact.com/digital-product-passport-software). The AWARE system for BGMEA uses a QR code per product — [Just Style](https://www.just-style.com/news/bgmea-aware-dpp-bangaldesh-garment/).
- RFID adoption figures are dated. A 2014 GS1 US survey found about 47% of items received by apparel and general-merchandise retailers carried RFID tags — [SupplyChainBrain](https://www.supplychainbrain.com/articles/21093-rfid-makes-great-inroads-into-apparel-general-merchandise-verticals). Auburn RFID Lab research shows inventory accuracy rising from about 65% to over 95% with RFID — [Badger Technologies summary](https://www.badger-technologies.com/resource/blog/rfid-in-retail.html). Old Navy, Academy Sports and Kroger rolled out RFID (June 2025) — [RetailWire](https://retailwire.com/discussion/is-rfid-reinventing-retail-selling-floors/).
- Vendors say QR destinations can be updated without reprinting labels — [QRStuff](https://www.qrstuff.com/feeds/service/qr-code-care-label).
- Zebra expects fashion and textile firms to need to publish DPP data by 2027 or early 2028 — [Zebra infographic](https://www.zebra.com/content/dam/zebra_dam/en/infographic/retail-infographic-digital-product-passports-en-us.pdf).

### Inferences
- For a hackathon demo, a **GS1 Digital Link–style QR** (GTIN, batch or serial) that resolves to the sealed provenance record is the realistic carrier. Bangladeshi factories already apply brand-supplied care labels and hangtags, often printed by trim and label suppliers such as Avery Dennison and Trimco, so the QR would most likely be printed by the brand's label vendor rather than the factory. This is an inference.
- RFID is a brand and retailer inventory decision. Bunon should be able to *emit* identifiers compatible with EPC/RFID encodings, not deploy hardware.

### Gaps
- No primary EU legal text was checked on whether the textile delegated act mandates a specific carrier (QR vs RFID vs NFC). Other research tracks should confirm this.
- No 2025–26 survey of item-level RFID or QR care-label penetration in apparel was found.
- No source was found on woven-label or embroidered QR durability or adoption for DPPs.
- No source was found on which carriers Bangladeshi factories physically apply today for EU brands.
