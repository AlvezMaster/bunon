## Q1. What are the exact judging components, weights and band descriptors, what separates the top band, and what evidence can a judge see?

### Takeaway
The event page and the FAQ answer "How will submissions be judged?" both give six components: Innovation 20, Technical Execution 20, Business Model (+Global Readiness) 20, Real-World Impact (+Ethical AI Compliance) 20, Scalability (+NRB Collaboration) 10 and Presentation 10. Each has its own score bands and the total is normalised to 0–100. Guide §11 instead groups the same themes into five components of 20 points each. In every version, the top band needs four things: production-grade engineering, measurable outcomes, documented ethical safeguards and global or investor readiness. "Working, well-structured AI" stops at 11–15.

### Inferences
**Crosswalk between the two rubrics.** Guide component 5 ("Responsible AI, Data Sovereignty & Presentation") maps to the event page's Ethical-AI half of Real-World Impact plus Presentation. Build the pitch and the submission so that every row below is visibly covered.

**Top-band "stack".** The Guide's 16–20 band needs three things at once: "globally competitive", "production-grade" and "documented safeguards". The event page's top bands add "real-world deployment", "high social return", "strong potential" and "investor-ready". A team can secure 11–15 with a clean working AI system. Reaching 16–20 needs proof artefacts: eval numbers, validation evidence, a safeguards document and a deployment pathway.

**Two audiences.** Judges score both the 3-minute live pitch (with 1 minute to score) and the full written submission. Every claim needs a visible artefact in both places: a screen in the video and a field or link in the submission.

### Gaps
- No official source explains how the six event-page components relate to the Guide's five-component rubric, or which one panels will actually use on 26 Dec. The FAQ adds a third "default rubric" (see Q8).
- No official worked examples or sample scored submissions exist.

## Q2. How is scoring aggregated, and how does judging run in practice?

### Takeaway
At least 5 independent judges score each team; the FAQ also states a minimum of 3. The FAQ says totals are averaged, with a trimmed mean once 4 or more judges score and an extra judge when judges differ by 25 points or more. On Congress Day each team gets a single 5-minute slot: 1 minute transition, 3 minutes pitch, 1 minute scoring. Only the locked submission as of the deadline is judged.

### Cited Findings
- "Each judge produces a weighted total normalized to 0–100 using the rubric weights. The final score is the average of judge totals across the minimum required judges (after excluding any conflict-of-interest judges). When 4 or more judges score, a trimmed mean is used — the highest and lowest are dropped before averaging — and scores are rounded to 2 decimals." — [FAQ › Judging & Awards](https://cloudcampbd.com/ai-builders-congress)
- On disagreement: "If the gap between the highest and lowest judge totals is 25 or more points, the submission is flagged High Variance and an additional judge is assigned". — [FAQ › Judging & Awards](https://cloudcampbd.com/ai-builders-congress)
- Tie-breaks, version 1: "higher average Innovation sub-score, then higher Technical Execution sub-score, then higher Real-World Impact sub-score, then earlier submission timestamp, and finally Admin manual resolution". — [FAQ › Judging & Awards](https://cloudcampbd.com/ai-builders-congress)
- Tie-breaks, version 2: "higher average Impact sub-score, then higher average Technical sub-score, then higher average AI & Data sub-score, then earlier submission timestamp". — [FAQ › Submission Process](https://cloudcampbd.com/ai-builders-congress)
- Congress-Day format. "Finalist teams are judged once, using a single, consistent evaluation… these are not two different rounds". "Per-team format: 5 minutes per team — 1 minute transition + 3 minutes pitch + 1 minute scoring". Group Session 1, 11:00 AM – 1:00 PM: "approximately 14 teams per panel". Group Session 2, 2:00 – 3:00 PM: "approximately 7 teams per panel". "Presentation flow: Structured demo: problem → solution → demo → AI → impact, followed by judge questions". "Scoring: Independent scoring against the unified rubric (Section 11), submitted immediately". — [Guide §9.3](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1rKlflRaDZW475JmeOgM_gS2z1lvivabL/edit). The event page schedule labels these sessions "Idea to Unicorn — Team Judging (Group 1 of 2)" and "(Group 2 of 2)". — [Event page › Program Schedule](https://cloudcampbd.com/ai-builders-congress)
- The Congress-Day schedule also has "9:00 – 11:00 AM Pre-Judging Round & Opportunity Connect — Early reviews and first 1:1 stakeholder meetings" and "8:30 – 9:00 AM Disruptive Innovation Showcase Tour". — [Guide §16](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1rKlflRaDZW475JmeOgM_gS2z1lvivabL/edit)
- After the final deadline: "Server-side lock triggers automatically. Judges are assigned within 24 hours, the judging window runs 25–26 December, aggregation and tie-breaking finish on the morning of Congress Day". "Only what is captured in the Locked submission is judged. Judges evaluate the version at deadline, not any post-deadline improvements." "Usually no — judging is based on the submission as it stands." — [FAQ › Submission Process](https://cloudcampbd.com/ai-builders-congress)
- On judges: "senior industry professionals, founders, researchers, NRB experts and international collaborators… masked from teams during evaluation". — [FAQ › Judging & Awards](https://cloudcampbd.com/ai-builders-congress)
- Overall score bands: "0–39 Emerging, 40–59 Promising, 60–74 Strong, 75–89 Excellent, 90–100 World-Class. Only Strong and above are typically eligible for named awards." — [FAQ › Judging & Awards](https://cloudcampbd.com/ai-builders-congress)
- Rankings: "Rankings are produced per event, per phase, and per domain by default, with optional per-challenge sub-rankings. Awards are given per domain plus overall Congress awards." Feedback: "each team receives per-criterion averages and combined qualitative feedback". — [FAQ › Judging & Awards](https://cloudcampbd.com/ai-builders-congress)
- Appeals: "within 48 hours of results being published… limited to procedural questions… not disagreement with subjective scores". — [FAQ › Submission Process](https://cloudcampbd.com/ai-builders-congress)
- Participation scale as of 9 Nov 2026: "4,914 participants", "884 teams registered". — [Event page header](https://cloudcampbd.com/ai-builders-congress)

### Inferences
- The trimmed mean drops the highest and lowest judges, so a few enthusiastic judges cannot carry the team. Unambiguous, low-variance evidence (numbers on screen, the named challenge's exact output) is worth more than flashy but debatable claims.
- Judges get one minute to score after a 3-minute pitch. Each component's evidence needs a clearly labelled moment: an on-screen caption or slide title per component helps judges map it.
- Under one tie-break order Innovation is the first tie-breaker; under the other it is Impact. Both orders put Technical second, so Technical Execution is a safe priority.

### Gaps
- Number of finalists, panels and judges per panel. The Guide implies about 21 teams per panel across the two sessions, but the number of panels is not stated.
- Whether the written-submission review (25–26 Dec) and the live pitch are combined into one score or weighted separately is not stated.

## Q3. Which domain or challenge fits a garment-supplier compliance tool, what does each candidate challenge ask for, and can a team define its own?

### Takeaway
Teams pick one domain and one challenge, or may propose their own idea within a domain under the same rubric. JusticeSphere AI → ComplianceOS fits Bunon best, with Risk Sentinel second. IndustrySphere AI → Supply Chain Nexus is a weaker fit because it asks for forecasting/optimisation and "Cost, resilience, and speed gains".

## Q5. What do the rules say about Bangla/localization, personalization, real data, Responsible AI and Data Sovereignty?

### Takeaway
Bangla/localization is a mandatory preliminary consideration, a named top-10 mistake when missing, part of the "Winning Formula", and part of the "Final Unified Requirement". Personalization and real data (RAG plus Graph) carry the same status. ### Inferences
**Bunon-specific risk register, in priority order.**
1. **Simulated issuer registry and unlabelled synthetic data.** These could be read as "fake or simulated results" or "misrepresent technical capabilities", which are disqualification risks, not just lost points. Label them visibly now and disclose them in provenance.
2. **Overbuilding.** The ledger, Merkle tree, passport, buyer terminal, MCP, audit logs and settings pages are many features (Mistake #7). ## Q8. Where do the Guide, the event page and the FAQ contradict each other, and which unofficial claims should be ignored?
